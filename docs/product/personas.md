# Provisional persona hypotheses

Updated: 2026-10-08

Teddy and Jenna are **provisional hypothesis personas supplied by the project owner**. They have not been interviewed or validated, and they do not yet satisfy the course's real-user-validation requirement. This document intentionally adds no invented quotes, countries, income, occupations, devices, or other personal facts.

## Persona 1: Teddy

### Personal situation

Teddy is a mid-teens high-school student and a tech-savvy movie lover. He has conversational English, learned mostly from watching movies.

### Technology and media habits

He downloads and watches many movies. He can download movies, find subtitle files, and search the internet for information. He hates slow internet and time-wasting tools.

### Problem

Science-fiction and historical dialogue creates a language barrier. Conversational English is not enough for him to understand all of the difficult dialogue.

### Goal

Teddy wants to understand the entire movie, including its difficult dialogue.

### What he needs from SRT Translator

He needs a direct way to load the English subtitle file he already found and translate it without a slow, wasteful workflow. The result needs to make difficult dialogue reviewable rather than hiding uncertainty.

### What success looks like

Teddy can translate and review the subtitle without unnecessary delay and can understand difficult parts of the movie more completely.

### Assumptions requiring real-user validation

- Whether an English-SRT-first workflow matches how Teddy actually watches movies.
- Which target language Teddy needs.
- Whether the current speed, reading profiles, warnings, and review flow save enough time for him.
- Whether joined continuation speech materially helps him understand science-fiction and historical dialogue.
- What level and kinds of translation error prevent him from understanding the whole movie.

## Persona 2: Jenna

### Personal situation

Jenna is in her early thirties and lives with her parents. Her family has a weekly movie night, and her parents do not understand English.

### Technology and media habits

She can download a movie, find an English subtitle, and upload it.

### Problem

English dialogue prevents her parents from fully following family movie night. An English subtitle alone does not solve that barrier.

### Goal

Jenna wants family movie night to include everyone. She wants a reasonably cohesive subtitle in the family's mother tongue so her parents can understand the movie's plot.

### What she needs from SRT Translator

She needs a translation that is cohesive enough for plot comprehension and a review/editing workflow for correcting important mistakes. She understands that automatic translation will not be professional or movie-theatre grade.

### What success looks like

Her parents can follow the movie's plot, and Jenna can find and correct important errors before the family relies on the subtitle.

### Assumptions requiring real-user validation

- Which mother tongue and movie genres Jenna's family needs.
- How much review time Jenna will accept before a weekly movie night.
- Which errors Jenna considers important enough to correct.
- Whether Jenna can reliably judge and repair the target-language output.
- What “reasonably cohesive” and sufficient plot comprehension mean to Jenna and her parents.

## Shared needs

- A simple upload-to-download workflow based on an English SRT they already know how to find.
- Translation that treats connected dialogue cohesively enough to support understanding.
- Honest feedback about waiting, failures, readability problems, and uncertain redistributed timing.
- A usable SRT that keeps the movie's original cue order and timing.
- A workflow that avoids hidden work loss and unnecessary repeated translation.

## Important differences

Teddy's primary aim is his own understanding of difficult genre dialogue, and his intolerance of slow or wasteful tools is explicit. Jenna's aim is an inclusive family activity: her parents need to follow the plot, and she expects to review and correct important mistakes. Jenna explicitly accepts that automatic output is below professional or cinema grade; no equivalent quality expectation has been supplied for Teddy.

## Validation still required

The project owner must test these hypotheses with real people and record interview or observation evidence, changes to the personas, and rejected assumptions. Until then, priorities, success measures, target languages, acceptable wait time, and translation-quality thresholds remain provisional.
